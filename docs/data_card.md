# Data card — UVERA-World (synthetic)

Every number below comes from `reports/data_card_stats.json`, `reports/world_meta.json` and
`reports/leakage_check_ai1.json`, written by notebook **NB00**. No real customer, agent or merchant data is used anywhere.

## What it is
A fully synthetic Bangladeshi mobile-money world generated by `ml/uvera_ml/sim/world.py` from
[`configs/assumptions.yaml`](../configs/assumptions.yaml) and [`configs/patterns.yaml`](../configs/patterns.yaml).
Seed **42**; every notebook regenerates the same world and checks its SHA-256 hashes, so all results are reproducible.

## How labels are made (and why the task is not trivial)
- Labels come from **hidden processes** (susceptible victims, mule wallets, merchant disguise families), never from a
  rule on a single feature.
- **Label noise:** 373 scam labels are lost (unreported) and 373 false reports are added (10%).
- **Honest look-alikes:** large transfers to new receivers (rent, family, Eid), fixed-price shops with round amounts,
  supplier payments.
- **Leakage guard:** no single AI-1 feature may separate scams with AUC above 0.80. Highest measured:
  `recipient_sender_days_7d` 0.781, `recipient_in_count_7d` 0.781, `amount_log` 0.739 → **pass**.

## Splits
| Split | Days | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 1–70 (after a 14-day warm-up for history features) | StratifiedGroupKFold inside (5 folds × 5 seeds) |
| Calibration | 71–85 | isotonic calibration |
| Validation | 86–100 | thresholds and model choice, then frozen |
| Test | 101–120 | used once; AI-1 test customers never appear in training |

Text (AI-2) is split by writing style (style B never seen in training); QR disguise family D is held out of training
entirely.

## Public data (tests only)
UCI SMS Spam Collection (CC BY 4.0): an external sanity test for AI-2 only, downloaded inside NB02 and not committed.

## Limitations
Behaviour, rates and patterns are assumptions informed by public reports, not any provider's data. Results on this world
show that the method works end to end; they are not real-world accuracy.
